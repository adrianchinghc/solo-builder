# Perpetual Launch Playbook

## The Biggest Launch Mistake

Most founders treat a launch like a single event. They work for months, ship, post to Product Hunt, get a spike of traffic, and then watch it die down over two weeks. They think they failed.

They didn't fail. They just stopped launching.

The core insight: The launch is never over. Every significant product update, every new feature, every spinoff product is a new launch opportunity. You're not announcing once — you're building a continuous narrative that keeps generating press, traffic, and user interest.

The launch is perpetual.

---

## What Counts as a Launch Moment

Not everything is launch-worthy. The question isn't "what did we ship?" — it's "what story does this let us tell?"

**Launch-worthy moments:**
- Version 2.0 or 3.0 of your product (significant feature additions or redesign)
- Entering a new market or use case
- Crossing a meaningful milestone (1,000 users, $10K MRR, 1M data points)
- Adding an entirely new product line or integration
- A surprising or counter-intuitive result from your product's data
- An operational change with a story ("We've been completely remote for 3 years, here's what we learned")

**Not launch-worthy (usually):**
- Bug fixes
- UI tweaks
- Minor feature additions
- Performance improvements (unless the numbers are dramatic)

## Make Every Feature a Launch

When you ship a significant feature, don't just push it to production and move on. Make it an event:

1. **Write a blog post or Twitter thread** about why you built it, what problem it solves, and what you learned
2. **Email your existing users** — they want to know the product is improving
3. **Reach out to 3–5 relevant journalists** with a 2-sentence pitch (see press-outreach-playbook.md)
4. **Post to relevant communities** (subreddits, Slack groups, Discord servers, niche forums)
5. **Tweet the milestone** with specific numbers if possible

This doesn't mean five separate big efforts. A blog post + email + 3 targeted journalist emails + 1 Reddit post = 2–3 hours of work. But it reactivates press coverage that compounds over time.

## Version Number Launches

Use version numbers as launch anchors:

- **v2.0** = major redesign, new core feature, or new business model
- **v3.0** = significant expansion to new audience or use case

These let you say "we're back" even on platforms that restrict relaunching the same product (like Product Hunt). A v2.0 is a new submission. Do this every 12–18 months as your product matures.

**Before a version launch:**
- Notify your email list 7 days before ("Something big is coming")
- Prep your Product Hunt submission (thumbnail, tagline, gallery) in advance
- Line up 3–5 journalist pitches to go out same day as the PH launch
- Have a changelog ready that clearly explains what changed and why it matters

## The Feature-as-Product Approach

Instead of adding features to a main product, launch them as independent products. Each gets its own Product Hunt launch, its own press cycle, its own user base.

**The mechanics:**
1. You build a feature that has value beyond your core user
2. Instead of burying it inside your app, you spin it out as its own domain and landing page
3. You launch it as a new product (Product Hunt, HN, Reddit, etc.)
4. It drives attention back to your main product
5. Later, you can either: keep it standalone, integrate it back, or sell it

**Side Project Marketing in Practice:**
- Crew (a freelance marketplace) built **Unsplash** (free stock photos) as a side project
- Unsplash grew bigger than Crew itself
- Then they built **HowMuchToMakeAnApp.com** — a simple calculator
- Each side project got massive press and drove traffic back to the core business

Similarly, Buffer launched Pablo (image editor) and Respond (social media customer support tool) as independent products rather than buried features — each became its own launch moment.

**What makes a good spinoff:**
- Solves one clear problem for a broader audience than your main product
- Can be explained in one sentence
- Requires no account or payment to use (free tools spread fastest)
- Has a memorable, specific name or domain

**The portfolio press flywheel:**
Side project 1 gets press → audience discovers main product → main product gets more users → generates insights → side project 2 is born → more press → repeat.

## Spinning Off a Feature

**Signs a feature is spinoff-worthy:**
- People outside your core user base would find it useful
- It could stand alone without the rest of your product
- You've seen it shared on social media more than any other feature
- It solves a universal problem that happens to also be relevant to your users

**How to spin off:**
1. Extract the feature into a standalone URL (or subdomain)
2. Write a simple landing page explaining what it does and who it's for
3. Add basic analytics and an email capture
4. Launch it as if it's a brand new product (because it is)

Examples of spinoffs that worked:
- Data dashboards → standalone public leaderboards
- Internal tools → developer APIs with a landing page
- Comparison features → standalone comparison sites ("X vs. Y")
- Calculators built for your industry → general tools for that industry

## The Evergreen Launch on Reddit

Reddit's search engine is old, and posts stay findable for years. A post in `r/digitalnomad` from 3 years ago about Nomads.com still drives organic sign-ups today.

**Strategy:** For each major launch, identify 3–5 subreddits where your product is genuinely useful. Write a post tailored to each subreddit's culture (not copied from the others). These posts live forever and compound.

Wait at least 6 months between significant posts in the same subreddit. Posting too often looks spammy and gets you banned.

## Keeping Multiple Products in the Air

Running multiple products simultaneously is viable. Most get 20% of attention each, with 2–3 getting the bulk.

**The portfolio mindset:**
- You don't need one massive product — a portfolio of small profitable products can add up to more than one big one
- Diversification protects against platform changes (algorithm updates, App Store policy changes, API deprecations)
- Multiple products mean multiple income streams and multiple launch opportunities per year

**Rules for managing multiple products:**
- Each product needs its own monitoring, automation, and contractor setup before you step back from it
- Never let a product go fully dark — someone (you or a contractor) should check in weekly
- If a product is generating $1K+/month with minimal maintenance, it's worth keeping alive even if it's not growing

## Email List: The Only Channel You Own

Every platform you launch on (Product Hunt, HN, Reddit, Twitter) can change its algorithm, ban you, or disappear. Your email list cannot be taken away.

On **every launch:**
1. Add a visible email capture above the fold ("Get updates when we launch new features")
2. Make the value proposition specific — not "subscribe to my newsletter" but "Get notified when new PHP developer jobs are posted" (for a jobs board)
3. Send a launch email to your existing list on launch day
4. Follow up 7 days later with what you learned from the launch

Your email list compounds across all launches. Each launch grows it. Each new product announcement reaches everyone who ever cared about anything you've built.

## The Motivation Test

Working on one product for 2–3 years is hard. Here's the honest truth about motivation and what to do with it:

**Keep building if:**
- Real users are engaging (even if small numbers)
- Revenue is moving, even slowly
- You're still learning something new from the problem space

**Kill it (or automate and ignore it) if:**
- Nobody uses it despite multiple launch attempts across different channels
- Revenue is flat or declining for 6+ months with no clear explanation
- You dread opening the codebase

**Sell it if:**
- It's profitable but you don't want to work on it anymore
- Someone else would do more with it than you would

A product that bores you is a liability. Kill, automate, or sell. Then ship the next thing.

## Do This Today

Look at the last 3 features you shipped. For each one, ask: "Did I tell anyone about this outside my existing user base?" If the answer is no — pick the most interesting one and write a 280-character tweet about what you built and why. That's the beginning of treating every feature as a launch.
