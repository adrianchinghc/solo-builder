# Press & Media Outreach Playbook

## Why Press Still Matters (And Why It Matters Less Than Before)

Press gets your product in front of mainstream users who don't browse Product Hunt or Hacker News. A feature in Forbes or TechCrunch can bring 50,000–200,000 visitors in a week. It adds credibility that self-published content can't replicate.

But press is declining in importance. People read less editorial content. Trust in media is low. The rise of Product Hunt, Twitter, and direct newsletter audiences has shifted where tech discovery happens. Treat press as a **supplementary channel** that amplifies your existing traction — not as the thing that creates it.

The exception: **niche industry press.** If you're building software for hairdressers, the main hairdresser industry trade publication is worth 10x more than a TechCrunch feature. Your specific audience actually reads it.

---

## The Central Mistake Everyone Makes

Emailing `editors@techcrunch.com` or `tips@theverge.com`. These inboxes are black holes. Hundreds of pitches a day, barely any are read.

The way to get press: **contact the right individual journalist directly, personally, with a 1–2 sentence pitch.**

---

## Building Your Press Spreadsheet

Before you pitch anyone, build a target list. This is the foundation.

**Step 1: Find journalists who cover your space**
- Search: "[competitor product name] TechCrunch" — who wrote about them?
- Search: "[your category] Forbes 2024" — who writes in this space?
- Use **Submit.co** — a directory of press outlets and journalists by category
- Check who's written about adjacent products in the last 12 months
- On Product Hunt, check which publications covered top products in your category

**Step 2: Build the spreadsheet**

| Name | Outlet | Niche/Beat | Email | Twitter | Emailed? | Replied? | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Susan Lee | TechCrunch | Consumer apps, pet tech | susan@... | @susanlee | Yes | Yes | Will feature |
| Mark Chen | Fast Company | Bootstrapped founders | mark@... | @markchen | Yes | No | — |

**Step 3: Work the numbers**
Expect less than 5% of targeted pitches to result in coverage. To get 5 press features, you need to contact 100+ journalists. Even well-targeted. That's the reality.

## Writing the Pitch Email

### The Golden Rule: 2 sentences max

Journalists skim. They don't read. Your pitch has 3 seconds. Everything that extends past 2 sentences is working against you.

**Bad pitch:**
```
Subject: New startup Petsy.com

Hi TechCrunch!

We just launched our new startup Petsy. We're growing like crazy and crushing 
it right now and we'd love to tell you more about our launch this week.

Petsy connects pet owners and pet food. It works like this, you go to the app, 
then you select which food you want. Then you tap order. It then delivers the 
food to your house. We have about 5,000 people in our app and growing 40%. 
Thanks for covering us!

Rick
CEO, Founder Petsy.com
```

**Why it fails:**
- Way too long
- Contacts a generic inbox (not a specific journalist)
- Vague bragging ("crushing it", "growing like crazy")
- No clear link to the product
- Subject is generic
- Buries the point in paragraph 3

**Good pitch:**
```
Subject: Food delivery startup for pets

Hi Susan! I made a site that lets you subscribe to food delivery for your 
pet. Let me know if you need more info :)

https://petsy.com
```

**Why it works:**
- 2 sentences
- Addressed to Susan specifically
- Friendly but not sycophantic
- The journalist can click and decide for themselves
- Subject is clear and specific
- Leaves room for them to ask for more

**Add a personal touch if you have one:**
```
Subject: Food delivery startup for your puppy Robert de Niro

Hi Jody! I saw you have a new golden retriever — I made a site that lets you 
subscribe to food delivery for your pet so you never run out.

https://petsy.com
```

Journalists talk publicly about their pets, kids, hobbies, opinions. If something connects your product to something they've mentioned, use it. It signals you did research instead of bulk-emailing.

## When They Reply

**Reply within 1 hour.** Journalists are often writing the article the same day. If they reply asking for more info and you respond 3 days later, the article doesn't happen. Check your email when you've sent pitches.

When they ask for data and screenshots:
- Be concise — answer what they asked, don't dump everything
- Send screenshots already cropped and labeled (make their job easy)
- Give specific numbers: "2,300 sign-ups, 180 paying customers at $29/month, launched 6 weeks ago"
- Don't say "we're growing like crazy" — give a percentage and a timeframe

Make the journalist's job easy and they'll write about you faster and more favorably.

## Controversy as Press Strategy

The news is called "news" because it covers what's *new* or *surprising*. If your story is the same as 100 other stories, it won't get written.

Pieter Levels launched **GoFuckingDoIt.com** — a site that charges your credit card if you don't hit your goal. The name alone guaranteed press. The premise — "I keep your money if you fail" — was the story.

**How to find your controversial angle:**

1. What does your product believe that most people (or competitors) don't?
2. What conventional wisdom does your approach challenge?
3. What would make someone uncomfortable — and why are you doing it anyway?

**Pieter's book pitch:** "VC is bad, indie is good." Simplified to the point of being controversial. Not literally true — but it captures a real insight in a memorable, quotable way. That framing got the book covered everywhere.

Your controversial angle doesn't have to be the product itself. It can be:
- Your business model ("I charge more when demand is higher and nobody apologizes")
- Your founder story ("I built this without any code or funding in 3 weeks")
- Your audience ("I made a $100 product for hairdressers doing African hair — and it's more profitable than the B2B SaaS I spent a year on")

**Rules for controversy:**
- Be honest — you must genuinely believe the position
- Simplify to the most striking version of the truth
- Be willing to defend it in follow-up questions
- Don't be controversial just to be controversial — it has to connect to real value

## Niche Press vs. Mass Tech Press

For most products, **niche industry publications outperform mass tech press.**

If you build software for:
- Hairdressers → haircare trade publications
- Restaurant owners → food service industry press
- Real estate agents → proptech and realtor publications
- Fitness coaches → fitness business publications

These audiences are exactly your customer. They trust their industry publications. The journalist knows your niche. A feature here means 1,000 readers who are all potential buyers vs. 100,000 TechCrunch readers who mostly aren't.

How to find niche press:
- Google: "[your industry] magazine", "[your industry] trade publication"
- Find what your target customers read on LinkedIn or in their communities
- Check what your competitors advertise in

## Building Warm Relationships

A journalist who's written about you before is 10x more likely to write about you again. These relationships are gold.

**How to build them:**
- After coverage, thank the journalist by name (not just a generic reply)
- Keep them updated on significant milestones (not every update — significant ones)
- Give them exclusive scoops occasionally: news you don't give anyone else first
- Don't bug them — one update per quarter is plenty unless you have real news

**Scoops as currency:** "I wanted to give you this first before I announce it publicly" is the most powerful phrase in press relations. Journalists value exclusives because it makes their coverage more compelling.

---

## When Not to Do Press Outreach

- When you have nothing to show yet (a landing page is not a product)
- When your product has major unresolved bugs
- When you need quick sales (press takes weeks; do direct sales instead)
- When you're in a media cycle that drowns out your story (major news events)

## Quick Reference: Tier 1 Tech/Business Press

| Outlet | Best for |
|---|---|
| TechCrunch | Funding news, product launches, founder stories |
| The Next Web (TNW) | European founders, tech culture, apps |
| Wired | Technical depth, society/tech intersection |
| Fast Company | Bootstrapped founders, design, work |
| Forbes | Business angle, revenue numbers, profiles |
| Inc. | Small business, entrepreneurship |
| Hacker News | Tech, tools, "Show HN" (community-driven, not journalist-pitched) |
| Product Hunt | App launches (community-driven, not journalist-pitched) |
