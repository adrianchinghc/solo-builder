# LinkedIn B2B Distribution Playbook

## When to Use LinkedIn

LinkedIn works best when:
- Selling to professionals with clear job titles (head of ops, VP eng, founder, CFO)
- ACV is high enough to justify manual outreach ($200+/yr per seat or $1K+/yr deals)
- Buyer is reachable by job function, company size, or industry
- You can help them before selling to them

LinkedIn is slower than Reddit or Twitter for consumer. It is one of the highest-converting channels for B2B.

---

## Profile: Personal vs Company Page

**Use personal profile first. Always.**

Company pages get organic reach near zero. A founder's personal profile gets 5–10x more visibility and builds trust faster.

### Personal Profile Setup (B2B)

- **Headline**: Don't use your job title. Use value: "I help [persona] [outcome] without [pain]"
  - Bad: "Founder at Acme"
  - Good: "Helping ops teams close books 3x faster without spreadsheet chaos"
- **Banner**: Product screenshot or one-liner tagline
- **About section**: 3–5 sentences. Problem → who you help → proof → CTA
- **Featured section**: Pin your best post, a case study, or a demo link
- **Creator mode**: Turn on. Adds "Follow" button instead of "Connect" — grows reach faster

### Company Page (Use Later)

Only create once you have:
- 100+ employees, OR
- Hiring and need legitimacy, OR
- Running LinkedIn Ads (required)

Don't post to company page until you have an audience strategy.

## Outreach: Connection Sequences

The goal is conversation, not a pitch. Never pitch in the first message.

### Connection Request (300 char limit)

Personalize. Reference something real.

Template: "Hey [name] — saw you're leading ops at [company]. Building something that [relevant outcome]. Would love to connect."

Never: "I'd like to add you to my professional network."

### Message Sequence After Connecting

**Day 1 (same day or next day):**
"Thanks for connecting, [name]. Quick question — are you still using [tool/process] for [specific workflow]? Curious what's working for teams like yours."

Ask a question they can answer in 2 sentences. Don't pitch.

**Day 4 (if no reply):**
"Sent a note a few days ago — no worries if timing was off. Still curious: what's your biggest headache with [relevant problem area] right now?"

**Day 8 (final):**
"Dropping by one more time. I've been building [one-line description] for [persona] — if this ever becomes relevant, happy to show you what it does. No pressure either way."

Pause after 3 messages. Don't send a 4th. If they reply at any point, have a real conversation.

### What to Say Once They Engage

- Ask about their current workflow, not "what do you need"
- Identify friction: "how long does that take you right now?"
- Offer a demo only after you understand their problem
- If they're a fit: "Want me to show you how [product] handles that?" — keep it frictionless

## Sales Navigator

Use it when you've validated your ICP and want to scale outreach.

### What It Does That Free LinkedIn Doesn't

- Filter by company size, headcount growth, seniority, function, geography simultaneously
- See who's changed jobs recently (buying window)
- Save leads to lists and track engagement
- TeamLink shows if anyone in your network knows the prospect

### Building a Lead List

1. Define ICP tightly: job title + company size + industry + geography
2. Add signals: recent job change, company funding round, or headcount growth (active buyers)
3. Limit first list to 100–200 leads (quality > quantity)
4. Work the list in batches of 20/week — slower and more personal converts better

### Who to Target First

- Job changers in the last 90 days (new budget authority, new problems to solve)
- Companies that just raised Series A/B (spending mode)
- Roles at companies using complementary tools (integration angle)

## LinkedIn Content Strategy for B2B SaaS

### Why Post on LinkedIn

- Warms cold prospects before outreach (they recognize your name)
- Inbound DMs from people who follow you before you reach them
- Builds authority in your space — prospects research you after hearing your name

### Content Types That Perform

**1. "What I learned" frameworks** (highest reach)
Short numbered lists from direct experience. No fluff. Specific.
Example: "5 things we got wrong onboarding our first 20 B2B customers"

**2. Problem/pain content** (best for ICP engagement)
Describe the problem your buyer faces in their words. Don't mention your product.
Example: "Most ops teams spend 8 hours/week on a report their CEO looks at for 4 minutes."

**3. Behind-the-scenes milestones**
Revenue, customer count, rejection stories. Build in public for B2B.
Keep it specific — "we just signed our 10th customer after 3 months of no" > "excited to share an update"

**4. Case studies (mini-format)**
3–5 sentences: before → what changed → after. No jargon.

### Posting Cadence

- 3x/week minimum to build momentum
- Best times: Tuesday–Thursday, 7–9am or 12–1pm in your target timezone
- Reply to every comment in the first hour — LinkedIn rewards it with reach

### Format Rules

- First line must hook: question, bold claim, or surprising stat
- No links in the body (LinkedIn suppresses reach) — put links in comments
- 3–5 lines max before "see more" — make the hook earn the click
- End with a question to drive comments

## Warm Outreach via Content

Before sending cold DMs, try this:
1. Follow and engage with prospects' posts for 1–2 weeks (genuine comments, not "great post!")
2. When you connect, you're already familiar — reply rates are 3–5x higher
3. Reference their post in your connection note

This is slower but converts far better than cold sequences.

## When to Add LinkedIn Ads

Only run LinkedIn Ads once:
- You have an ICP you've validated through manual outreach
- CAC from organic is below your LTV (so you have a ceiling to stay under)
- You have a converting landing page (organic traffic converts there first)

LinkedIn CPCs are expensive ($5–15/click). Don't spend until you know what converts.

Strongest ad formats for B2B SaaS:
- **Lead gen forms** (native, lower friction than landing page)
- **Thought leader ads** (promote your best organic post as a paid ad)
- Avoid: video ads (expensive), brand awareness (unmeasurable at early stage)

## Metrics to Track

| Metric | Why it matters |
|--------|---------------|
| Connection acceptance rate | >30% means your request copy is working |
| Reply rate to first message | >15% is strong; <5% means change the opener |
| Conversation-to-demo rate | How many conversations turn into calls |
| Content impressions/week | Leading indicator of audience growth |
| Inbound DM rate | Signals your content is driving trust |

---

## Common Mistakes

- Pitching in the connection request (instant ignore)
- Sending the same template to 500 people with no personalization
- Posting only about your product (zero reach, zero engagement)
- Creating a company page before a personal brand
- Using LinkedIn Ads before validating conversions organically
- Giving up after 2 weeks — LinkedIn compounds slowly, then fast
